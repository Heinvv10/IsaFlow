## Why this question is coming up now

Plenty of bookkeepers have tried pasting a trial balance into an AI chat to ask "what's going on here?" It works, sort of. It also means copying client data into a tool with no link back to the books, no audit trail and no idea whether the numbers are current.

MCP is a cleaner answer. Instead of copying data out, you let an assistant connect to the accounting system directly, with the access you choose.

## What MCP actually is

Anthropic introduced the Model Context Protocol on **25 November 2024** as "a new standard for connecting AI assistants to the systems where data lives" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol)). It's open-source. The architecture is simple:

- An **MCP server** exposes data and actions from a system, such as an accounting package.
- An **MCP client** is the AI application that connects to it, such as Claude, Cursor or Grok Bot.
- The server decides what the client can see and do.

That last point is the whole game. The protocol doesn't make an assistant trustworthy. The permissions on the server do.

## What an assistant should do in your books

The best uses are the ones where a mistake costs you a few minutes, not a SARS penalty.

### Reading and explaining

- "Summarise the trial balance at 30 September and flag anything unusual compared with August."
- "What's our VAT position for the current period, and what are the five biggest input tax claims?"
- "Which customers are more than 60 days overdue?"

These are questions a bookkeeper answers every week. An assistant can get to a first draft in seconds, and a person can check it.

### Preparing, not posting

- "Draft the month-end accrual journal for the audit fee."
- "Prepare a journal to reclassify the R12 400 in suspense to repairs and maintenance."

The assistant prepares a **draft**. It stays unposted for you to review. The agent can only post if you grant a separate posting permission, and we recommend keeping posting with a person.

## What an assistant shouldn't do

- **Skip the review.** Whoever prepares an entry, a person should review it before it's posted. That's basic segregation of duties, and it applies to software as much as to people.
- **File returns.** A VAT201 or EMP201 is a declaration to SARS. A registered person should review and submit it. See the [VAT201 guide](/blog/vat201-tax-periods-common-errors) and the [EMP201 guide](/blog/emp201-due-dates-penalties) for what's at stake.
- **See more than it needs.** Access should be per company and limited to the task.

## Why the VAT position is a good first question

VAT is where small errors turn into big numbers. In 2024/25, SARS collected **R561.4 billion** in domestic VAT payments and paid **R365.5 billion** in refunds, from **496 858 active vendors** out of **900 285 registered** ([SARS Tax Statistics 2025](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/tax-statistics/)).

## The controls to insist on

Whatever accounting system you use, ask these questions before you connect an assistant.

### 1. Is it read-only by default?

Reading is low-risk. Writing isn't. Start read-only, and only add more when you have a reason.

### 2. Does a person review before anything is posted?

Drafts are fine. We recommend that posting stays a person's decision, after review. In ISAFlow, posting needs a permission you choose to grant. Check which permissions the connection asks for, and grant only the ones you need.

### 3. Is access per company?

For a practice running many clients, an assistant connected to one client must not be able to see another.

### 4. Is there an audit trail?

There should be a record of what the assistant did, and a way to cut it off. In ISAFlow, every AI request is logged in an append-only audit log. You can list the drafts an assistant has created, and revoke its access at any time.

### 5. What about POPIA?

Your books hold personal information about employees and customers. The Protection of Personal Information Act requires reasonable security safeguards (section 19), and a written contract with any operator that processes personal information for you (section 21) ([POPIA, gov.za](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act)). Check what the AI provider does with the data it receives, and whether that fits your contracts with clients.
