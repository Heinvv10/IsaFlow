**TL;DR**

- A typical payroll-and-VAT client generates about **32 SARS and UIF submissions a year**. At 100 clients, that's **3 200** deadlines.
- You don't scale that by working harder. You scale it with **one standard close**, a **deadline calendar** across all clients, and **approval workflows** so nothing goes out unchecked.
- Anyone in your team who gives tax advice or completes returns for clients must be **registered as a tax practitioner**, or work under the direct supervision of someone who is.
- To your clients you're a POPIA **operator**. They need a written contract with you, and you need one with your software provider.

## The maths of a bureau

Start with what a single client asks of you, if you run their payroll and their VAT and they file VAT every two months:

| Submission | Per year |
| --- | --- |
| EMP201 (monthly) | 12 |
| UIF declarations (monthly) | 12 |
| VAT201 (Category A or B) | 6 |
| EMP501 (interim and annual) | 2 |
| **Total** | **32** |

That's before provisional tax, annual financial statements, CIPC returns or anything else you do.

The work isn't going away. The question is whether you can take on more of it with the same team.

## 1. Standardise the close

Every client should follow the same monthly close, in the same order. That's the single biggest lever.

A standard monthly close might be:

1. **Inputs in by day X.** Payroll changes, supplier invoices, bank statements. Publish the date to clients.
2. **Bank reconciled** using the [month-end bank reconciliation routine](/blog/bank-reconciliation-month-end-sme).
3. **Payroll run and reconciled** to the ledger.
4. **EMP201 prepared, reviewed and submitted.**
5. **VAT reviewed** in VAT months.
6. **Management pack** sent.

Write it down. A checklist that lives in someone's head can't be delegated, and it walks out the door when they do.

### Standardise the chart of accounts too

If every client has a different chart of accounts, every review starts from scratch. Use a standard chart with a small number of client-specific accounts. Reviewers learn where to look, and bank rules can be reused across clients.

## 2. Run one deadline calendar for every client

You need to see, on one screen, every client and every deadline: who's done, who's in review, who's stuck waiting for documents.

The columns are simple:

- client,
- obligation (EMP201, VAT201, EMP501 and so on),
- period,
- due date,
- preparer,
- reviewer,
- status.

Sort by due date. Everything due this week that isn't "submitted" is your morning stand-up.

## 3. Split preparer and reviewer

The person who prepares a return shouldn't be the only one who checks it. A two-step workflow catches errors and builds capacity, because:

- **preparers** can be junior and work through volume,
- **reviewers** focus on exceptions, judgement and sign-off,
- clients get a consistent standard whoever is on leave.

Track review findings. If the same error keeps coming back from one preparer, that's a training conversation. If it comes from every preparer, the checklist is wrong.

## 4. Get the compliance basics right

### Tax practitioner registration

Under [section 240 of the Tax Administration Act](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/faq-when-must-tax-practitioners-register-with-a-recognised-controlling-body/), every natural person who gives advice on a tax Act, or completes or helps complete a return for someone else, must:

- register with or fall under a **recognised controlling body**, and
- register with **SARS as a tax practitioner**,

within **21 business days** of first doing that work. Section 240(2) sets out exceptions. One covers a person who gives the advice or completes or helps complete a return solely under the direct supervision of a registered tax practitioner ([SARS FAQ on exemptions](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/faq-does-the-taact-exempt-any-person-from-registering-as-a-tax-practitioner/)). Check your team's status, especially for new hires.

### POPIA: you're an operator

Your clients are responsible parties for their employees' and customers' personal information. When you process it for them, you're an **operator**. Under [POPIA](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act), section 21 requires a written contract between them and you that ensures you apply the security safeguards in section 19, and you must notify the client immediately if you suspect personal information has been accessed by an unauthorised person.

In practice that means:

- an engagement letter or data processing agreement with every client,
- access controls, so staff only see the clients they work on,
- a breach procedure you've actually tested,
- the same checks on your own software provider.

## 5. Make the software do the predictable work

The point of software in a bureau isn't to replace judgement. It's to remove the work that doesn't need judgement:

- **Bank rules** for recurring lines, reused across clients.
- **Matching** that suggests the obvious pairs and leaves only exceptions.
- **Document capture** that turns a supplier invoice into a draft entry for a person to check.
- **Payroll** calculated from current tax tables, so nobody updates rates by hand.
- **Separate books per client**, so nothing leaks between companies.
- **Approval workflows**, so nothing goes out without a second check.

## 6. Measure what matters

Three numbers tell you whether you're scaling:

- **Clients per preparer.** It should rise over time without errors rising.
- **Days from inputs received to submission.** Shorter means less deadline stress.
- **Review rework rate.** The share of returns that bounce back from review.

If clients per preparer rises and rework rises with it, you're stretching, not scaling.

## FAQ

### How many clients can one payroll administrator handle?

It depends on payroll complexity, employee numbers and how standardised your process is. Measure clients per preparer and review rework, and grow the first without the second.

### Do bookkeepers need to register as tax practitioners in South Africa?

Anyone who gives advice on a tax Act or completes returns for others must register with a recognised controlling body and with SARS, unless an exception applies, such as doing the work solely under the direct supervision of a registered tax practitioner.

### What does POPIA mean for a payroll bureau?

You're an operator for your clients. You need a written contract with each client and must apply appropriate security safeguards and report breaches.

### How many SARS submissions does a payroll client need each year?

A client with monthly payroll and bi-monthly VAT needs about 32: 12 EMP201s, 12 UIF declarations, 6 VAT201s and 2 EMP501s.
