# Shared forensic controls

## Purpose
Shared accounting control contracts, canonical operation material and authenticated transaction context. MCP and REST adapters must use the same executing services. The programme ledger records which enforcement paths are implemented; types alone do not enforce accounting controls.

## Caller contract
- Obtain ControlTx with requireControlTx inside withAuth/withBearerAuth; assertControlTx rejects forged or escaped transaction contexts.
- ControlPrincipal comes from authenticated middleware, never tool/body arguments. Person identity differs from token/grant identity.
- Current permissions must be rechecked by executing services; grantedScopes is only a ceiling.
- Keep all financial effects, operation versions and committed change evidence in one transaction/savepoint. A controlWriteFailed flag prevents middleware COMMIT even if the handler catches a failure.

## Main interfaces
- contracts.ts: ControlPrincipal, ControlTx, OperationBinding, OperationApproval, ChangeEventInput, ControlCheckResult.
- canonical.ts: canonicalJson rejects lossy evidence; canonicalMoney preserves bounded decimal precision; operationBindingHash normalizes and binds all material facts.
- bindingValidation.ts validates dates, positive versions, policy and beneficiary/source references before hashing.
- context.ts creates immutable authenticated principals and binds control contexts to the live ALS client.
- auditCheckRunContract.ts / auditCheckRunJobService.ts: complete-population check runs as `audit_check_run` jobs (one intent per check, key `${userKey}:${checkId}`, name-based runGroupId, company quota 6 active, 100 per trailing 24 h, 40 per member). Limits come from the T1 spike; permissions are derived from the check, never stored authority. Migration 731 owns the result/finding tables.
- journalRiskShared.ts: the ONE missing-reasons loop, finding builder and coverage constant. Both `checkJournalRisks` and the check-run adapter call these; a private copy in either is a second truth and fails the shared-detector mutation gate.
- journalRiskPopulationSql.ts: one builder for both readings of the journal population. `slabTuple:false` keeps the paged detector's row shape (and therefore `sourceHash`) byte for byte; `slabTuple:true` adds the day/person/currency slab columns and orders by them for the cursor. Both LATERALs stay whole, so the cursor issues no per-parent child query.
- auditCheckRunPhase.ts / auditCheckRunStream.ts / auditCheckRunWriter.ts: phase A — one repeatable-read transaction with `SET LOCAL work_mem`, a NO SCROLL cursor, slab assembly across FETCH pages, batched sealed findings (512 rows / 2 MiB, RETURNING identity verified), an in-transaction re-read of what it wrote, the sealed header and the lease-fenced success UPDATE, all in one COMMIT.
- auditCheckRunUnknown.ts: the resource-limit path — `unknown`, no findings, no totals, population recounted in a FRESH read-committed transaction under the same lease (`populationCountBasis: 'recount_after_limit'`).
- auditCheckRunReadService.ts / auditCheckRunProtection.ts: `getAuditCheckRunSummary` (verdict, no findings) and `getAuditCheckRunPage` (≤100 findings) both return `CheckRunResult`; reader and seal domain `audit-check-run-header` / `audit-check-run-item`. The reader re-asserts the caller's CURRENT permissions, pages findings by ordinal bound to the published generation and lease, verifies every seal, and refuses a page whose stored finding census differs from the header.

## Business rules
- Tenant predicates on every tenant query, tagged SQL only. Unknown authority fails closed.
- Each applicable policy must pass; person separation is based on users.id, not token inequality.
- Sort set-valued references by fixed code-unit order; do not use locale collation for signatures.
- Never put plaintext bank/payroll secrets or tokens into generic audit output.
- Legacy missing provenance stays unknown; do not fabricate historical evidence.
- Retained rows are matched by canonical entity identity: the stored generated column `entity_id_canonical` (`lower(entity_id)`, migration 735) within `company_id`, and result identities compared with `sameIntentId`. Never match `lower(entity_id)` in a lookup: `lower()` is not leakproof and these tables FORCE RLS, so as app_user it is never an index condition and filters every row of the company (825 ms a journal on the risk population at 1M change events). The same holds for `entity_id_canonical = i.id::text` in a plain JOIN or pulled-up EXISTS — the uuid->text cast is not leakproof — so where another relation's row selects the canonical row, put the canonical table in a `LATERAL (... OFFSET 0)` (or a correlated subquery) so `i.id` arrives as a Param; where the receipt drives and the intent is joined to it, the intent LATERAL adds `i.id = CASE WHEN c ~ CANONICAL_UUID_TEXT THEN c::uuid END` beside `i.id::text = c` (`intentIdentity.ts`), a primary-key probe. Gates: `controlCanonicalIndexPath` / `controlCanonicalJoinPath` (plans as app_user) and the unit source guard `canonicalEntityLookupGuard` over src/, pages/ and scripts/ (only `aggregateOperationPolicy`'s exposure filter may compare `lower(entity_id)`). The guard is lexical: it cannot see a comparison built by string/template concatenation, nor tell a plain-join `entity_id_canonical = x.id::text` from the same text in a LATERAL — only the plan gates catch those, and only for the services they enumerate, so a new join site needs a plan-gate case. A receipt read (`loadIntentReceipt`) refuses two spellings of one receipt.
- A retained spelling is a lineage, and a fork (two spellings of one entity) is two lineages. Writers and projections follow one rule, `lineageChoice`/`retainedLineage`: the most advanced lineage (highest version), the canonical lowercase spelling on a tie; a top-version tie with no canonical spelling is refused by writers and named as an unresolvable tie wherever a fork is reported (below), since the entity stays blocked until someone adjudicates it. That lineage holds the entity's current state and its version-keyed rows (payroll calculation versions), so extending another strands real work. The caller's spelling never decides. The change event and protected evidence are written under the lineage the version row keeps.
- Callers that read versions with their own query (bankRuleChangeControl, glAccountChangeControl, glMergeChangeControl, defaultAccountsControl) match `entity_id_canonical` and choose through `retainedLineage`, so the version read and the row extended are always the same lineage.
- Projections that report evidence (audit snapshot sources, risk and three-way-match queries, payroll control) join versions through a `LATERAL ... ORDER BY version DESC, canonical first LIMIT 1`, and receipts through one that prefers the receipt that consumed the approval — never a bare equality on `entity_id_canonical`, which returns a forked entity twice, kills the snapshot job and inflates risk populations. A forked snapshot record carries `control_lineage_forked: true`, an unresolvable tie also `control_lineage_tie_unresolvable: true`, and a twinned attestation receipt `control_receipt_twinned: true` (only those records; others are unchanged), with `forked_lineage:<source>` / `unresolvable_lineage_tie:<source>` / `twinned_receipt:<source>` in `missingSources` — gaps a reviewer reads, never coverage. The risk, three-way-match, statement-completeness and payroll-control LATERALs report the chosen lineage's version and flag neither forks nor ties; the snapshot of those entities does. Statement completeness fails on a twin receipt with a `control_receipt_twinned` finding naming every receipt: a double execution is a control failure, not missing evidence. Transaction evidence flags `forked` and records `CONTROL_LINEAGE_FORKED`, or `CONTROL_LINEAGE_AMBIGUOUS` when no lineage can be followed. Detectors (journal risk) never pick a lineage: they read change history across every retained spelling and name the fork (`change_history_lineage_forked`) and the tie (`change_history_lineage_tie_unresolvable`), both counted from `control_entity_versions` as writers read it, so no signal depends on the spelling an edit was recorded under. Change history never merges lineages: on a fork it answers `FORKED_LINEAGE` with each lineage, `writerLineage` (the one the next write extends, chosen from `control_entity_versions` alone as writers choose) and `lineageTieUnresolvable` (writers refuse the entity), and each lineage is read, paged and cursored by name; a cursor minted before lineage selection is refused on a fork.
- Max 300 lines per implementation file; root owns shared contracts and migration allocation.
- Company lockdown is judged by `lockdownBlock` in `../gl/postingPeriodGuard.ts` and nowhere else; lockdown enabled with NO date locks EVERY date, payroll compensation included (`payrollCompensationWindows.ts` judges the first affected payroll day, not the operation date). Production may still hold companies saved as enabled+NULL by the legacy settings writer (`companyService.ts` before `validateLockdownTarget`): when this ships, every posting for such a company is refused until an approved `company_lockdown.change` gives it a date or turns it off. The deploy runbook's pre-check is `SELECT id,name FROM companies WHERE lockdown_enabled AND lockdown_date IS NULL` — run it on production before deploying and hand the list to Hein; isaflow_dev held 0 of 2 950 on 2026-09-11.
- Approvals and decisions are guarded in the database for every role, BYPASSRLS included (migrations 736/737, SQLSTATE 23514, messages `Approval birth|transition|decision refused: …`). The actor is `auth.uid()` (request.jwt.claims sub, = principal.personId on the cookie, bearer, impersonation and platform-import paths); no acting person, no request, decision or cancel. An approval is born by its requester, pending (approved only when `binding.policies` is `[]`). Edges: pending→pending|approved needs the acting person's approved decision recorded in the same transaction (and, for approved, an independent approved decision on every bound policy); pending→rejected their rejected decision; pending|approved→cancelled only the requester; approved→consumed names an effect committed in this transaction for the same company/operation/entity/operation_id/binding_hash, unclaimed. Every edge needs an unexpired approval; rejected/cancelled/consumed are frozen; identity, binding, requester, requested_at and expires_at never change (a cancel, a decision-less reject or a shorter expiry would release reserved aggregate exposure). A decision is append-only, by the acting person, a company member who is not the requester, on a pending unexpired approval, for a bound policy, once per policy (PK). A new write path to either table needs the triggers changed first. Tests: `asPerson` acts as a real person for synthetic shapes; `rewriteApprovalUnguarded` (superuser, `session_replication_role=replica`) only for states no transaction can reach now — legacy rows, corrupted rows, elapsed time.
- Company authority lock: `'control-authority:' || company` is taken EXCLUSIVELY by every function that resolves or checks a person's authority — `control_lock_permission_principal` (every `loadPermissionState`, so every `assertEffectivePermission`/`resolveEffectivePermissions` and every MCP call), `control_approval_authorities`, `control_beneficiary_authorities`, `control_exception_owner`, `platformImportAuthority.currentStanding`. It serialises every permission-checked transaction in a company from its first check to COMMIT; the readers' row locks (FOR UPDATE on the person's `company_users`/`users`, FOR SHARE on overrides, grant and company) are what keep an authority change from landing between a check and its action. Every holder waits at most 5 s (function-level `lock_timeout`, migration 738, which refuses to run past an unclassified function naming the key); a longer wait is 55P03, answered as 503 + `Retry-After` (`src/lib/lockContention.ts`), never 500 and never an unbounded queue. So any request that holds the key for more than 5 s makes every other same-company request 503. **Rule: a request must not hold the authority key longer than half its 5 s bound (2.5 s); bulk sizes are set from measured per-element holds** — `src/lib/limits/authorityHoldLimits.ts` (posted documents 15, finalized invoices 12, bank lines 6, recurring templates per run 6, payments per batch 8 — each keeping the WORST measured hold under 2.5 s; `authorityBulkMeasure`, `authorityBulkLimits` (gate) and `authorityLongHoldMeasure`), and a new path that runs many elements in one transaction needs a measured cap before it ships. Capped: bulk_finalize_invoices, bulk_approve_supplier_invoices, bulk_approve_credit_notes, bulk_allocate_bank_transactions, reverse_bank_allocation_batch, reverse_allocations, supplier-invoices-bulk-action, recurring-invoices-run (rounds; the page loops on `remaining`), batch-payments (at creation). **Known long holders, measured on the fixture at load 35-55 and left uncapped because one request IS the unit of work — Hein decides**: `run-depreciation` (REST) posts the WHOLE register in one request at ~0.21-0.25 s per asset (10 assets 1.4-2.6 s; already-posted assets are skipped, so a cap with re-run would keep its semantics — not done); a payment batch created before the cap processes whole at ~0.16-0.23 s per payment (100 payments ≈ 20 s); `import_bank_statement`/`bank-transactions-bulk-commit` hold ~15-45 ms for 10 lines with no per-line key cost but are unmeasured at the thousands of rows a 1 MB statement carries; the bank-rule auto-post sweep (up to 500 candidates per rule, each a posting) could not be measured: on this branch `applyRules` fails every auto-post line with `Generated journal must be changed through its authorized domain operation` and the route answers CONTROL_INTEGRITY_FAILED 500 — a pre-existing defect, reported. Flat and fine: `calculate_pay_run` 80-135 ms for 10 employees (no per-employee key cost), `post_payroll_journal` 0.2-0.5 s (one journal), recurring-journals-action, close-run, batch-payments-action (one posting per request). No key at all: gl-import, customers-bulk, statement-bulk-email, afs-mapping/bulk, practice bulk. The function-level `lock_timeout` also bounds the FOR UPDATE on the person's `users` row, which is NOT company-scoped: a member of two companies whose long transaction in company B holds their `users` row makes their own check in company A answer 503 after 5 s (nobody else's). The measurement suites (`authorityHoldMeasure`, `authorityBulkMeasure`, `authorityLongHoldMeasure`) are in the integration glob and SKIP unless their env var is set; they are not excluded. Shared readers were evaluated and DEFERRED: the exclusive key also hides every lock-order inversion between two permission-checked transactions, and a shared key exposed one at once — `legacyVoidControls` "adapter and direct approved cancellation share policy-before-payment lock order" deadlocked (40P01) because the adapter could check its own permission and lock the payment row while the direct caller held the approval participants and the policy lock.
- Audit jobs do NOT hold the key during their work. `auditJobRunner` claims, then resolves and asserts the job's delegated authority in its own short read-committed transaction (`pinAuditJobAuthority`), then opens the work transaction with that pin: `loadPermissionState` still checks the lease and delegation expiry live, then answers from the pin, taking neither the key nor the principal's rows. A job runs under the authority valid when it started: a revocation committed before the pin transaction fails it `AUTHORITY_REVOKED`, one committed during the run does not change its outcome, and every reader of the output re-authorises at read time (snapshot, export, comparison and check-run readers assert the caller's CURRENT permissions). `withControlJobRequest` without a pin (claim, `unknown` publication, direct callers) stays a live check. A 40P01/55P03 in the pin or work transaction requeues the job (retryable `WORKER_ERROR`).
