# services/packs/

## Purpose
Turns a `ReportPack` (from `reports/reportPackService.ts`) into the ONE document model every pack surface renders: the interactive screen, the printed PDF page, and the PowerPoint deck. Pure — no database, no React.

## Caller contract
`buildPackDocument(pack, trend)` has TWO callers: `usePackPrintData.ts` (the `?print=1` page, and therefore the PDF) and `pages/api/accounting/packs/[id]/pptx.ts` (the deck). The interactive screen `report-packs.tsx` does NOT build a document — it renders the same section blobs one level lower, through `reports/statementRows/packMappers.ts` plus `marginKvItem` from here. Both routes still lead back to the same mappers, so the three surfaces agree by construction, but a change to `buildPackDocument` alone reaches only two of them.

- `getPackTrend(companyId, to, months): Promise<PackTrendPoint[]>` (the one DB reader here)
- `packPrintApiPaths(type): string[]` · `packPagesApiPath(type)` · `packDownloadFilename(company, type, from, to, ext)`

## Business rules
- **Figures are COPIED, never derived.** These mappers read the section blob the pack service produced and translate keys; they compute no totals, no margins and no ratios. A figure that needs computing belongs in `reports/`, so the packs and the statements agree by construction rather than by coincidence.
- **A KPI's `value` is `number | null`, and null is the NORMAL case for an insolvent company.** AN-07: every equity-divided ratio is meaningless on non-positive equity, and scoring them 0 once rated such a company green. Every surface goes through `scorecardFigure` (or `formatScorecardValue` for the deck) — the screen wrote its own tile, typed the value `number`, and called `.toFixed(2)`, so expanding "KPI Scorecard" on Brightsphere threw and took the whole page to the error boundary. An unscored ratio also gets a NEUTRAL colour, never red: it did not fail, it could not be computed.
- `null` survives the mappers rather than defaulting to 0. A total of zero is a real total; a MARGIN of zero on no turnover is a percentage nothing on the page supports. `marginKvItem` prints `NO_MARGIN_REASON` ("No margin on non-positive turnover") instead. Every surface uses that one helper — two call sites formatting a nullable margin their own way is how one of them printed "0.0%" again.
- A section type with no blocks THROWS (`UnknownPackSectionError`). A titled, blank page in a document a director forwards to a bank is worse than a failed render.
- **A section blob missing a field THROWS too** (`MissingPackFieldError`, `packSectionData.ts`). `PackSection.data` is `Record<string, unknown>`, so the non-statement sections — cash flow, KPIs, the profit bridge, the ratio reasons, the commentary — used to be read by string key with a `num()` / `?? []` / `?? ''` fallback. Rename `operatingCashFlow` upstream and the cash-flow statement became six labelled zeros: plausible, uncompilable-against, and invisible to the "invents no figure" test, because 0 is already in the pack's own number set. A zero the ledger produced is a real figure; a zero a rename produced is a lie, and once a default has been applied nothing can tell them apart.
- `packPrintApiPaths` is the authority for what the print token authorises. A path the print page fetches that is missing from it is silently stubbed by the Playwright transport and 401s under Gotenberg — either way the PDF ships showing a loading state. Pinned by `tests/unit/components/packs/packPrintFetchParity.dom.test.tsx`. **It now has two branches, because there are two printed pages**: `board` fetches ONE path — the composed document from `packs/board/pages` — while `management`/`monthly` still fetch `reports-pack` + `trend`. Adding the legacy pair back to the board branch would authorise fetches that page never makes.
- **The board pack crosses a JSON boundary, and `TrustedChartSvg` does not.** R-P41's "composed in the same process that renders it" is retired: `renderChartSvg` needs `node:crypto` and ECharts, and the client webpack fallback sets `crypto: false`, so composition MUST happen on the server. `packDocumentWire.ts` is the whole boundary — `serialisePackPagesDocument` on the way out (the provenance class becomes a flat figure list; a `Map` would serialise to `{}`), `adoptPackPagesDocument` on the way in. Every SVG-bearing field (`PackChartSpec.svg`, `PackKpiTile.sparklineSvg`, `PackScorecardRow.trendSvg`/`.bulletSvg`) is re-branded through `adoptTrustedChartSvg`, which re-runs both guards; there is no `as TrustedChartSvg` cast in that file and there must never be one. The block switch there is also the runtime validator that the component tree no longer has.
- **`buildBoardPackDocument` fetches; `buildBoardPackPages` composes.** The composer stays synchronous, and its inputs are values. The fetches are SEQUENTIAL — `withAuth` holds one pg client in the async-local store and concurrent queries on it are a known hazard. `ledgerVersion` converts `getLedgerPostedThrough`'s deprecated 1970 sentinel to `null` (R-P46: page 12 prints "unavailable" rather than an invented audit anchor), and the draft status comes from `pages/packDraftState.ts` — **the only place that reads it** — never re-derived at a call site.
- **Three chart paths, and they are not the same picture.** `packChartOptions.ts` builds ECharts options that `PackChart` renders in the browser with the SVG renderer — that is what the screen shows and what the PDF photographs, which is why the print readiness gate waits for every chart's `<svg>` to exist. The DECK does not use them: `pptxExportService` calls `addChart` for a NATIVE PowerPoint chart, so a recipient can click into the data rather than receive a picture of it. A third path, `afs/serverChartSvg.ts`, serves NEITHER: it renders ECharts to an SVG string for the statutory AFS PDF, whose renderer runs no JavaScript. It used to sit in this directory, but it imports three `afs/` modules and its only consumer is `afs/pdfExportService.ts`, so it now lives beside that consumer; all it still takes from here is `DEFAULT_CHART_PALETTE`, so an AFS chart and a pack chart are the same colours. Changing a pack chart's appearance means changing `packChartOptions.ts` and `packDeckModel.ts`'s series, in two places, on purpose.

- **A pack that does not reconcile is not rendered.** `figures/packFigureIntegrity.ts` (`assertPackIntegrity`, with `packStatementIntegrity.ts` and `packWaterfallIntegrity.ts` beside it) runs as the LAST act of `buildBoardPackPages` and THROWS — spec §7.2. It checks that every printed figure resolves through the provenance map at the value the page carries, that one source renders identically everywhere (`assertConsistent`, exact by R-P27), that assets equal liabilities plus equity and each derived subtotal equals its terms, that every bridge chart's bars reach the endpoint it claims, that every percentage recomputes from its own numerator and denominator, and that a `prior` column is filled from `.prior` readings. Tolerances are one cent on money and one basis point on a percentage; a `null` figure with an `unavailable` reason is SKIPPED, never failed. Two things it deliberately does NOT do: sum a table's line rows into its subtotal row (a statement prints cost of sales positive and then subtracts it, so that formulation fails every correct income statement), and reject a bridge that foots on a NAMED plug — page 8's `Unexplained movement` and page 6's `Other, not separately captured` are both there because the figure set carries no reconciling items, and a plug a director can see beats a gap they cannot.
- **Page 11's quantum is a `PackFigure`.** It was a raw rand on the argument that an exposure assembled from four reports is not a ledger balance with a source to trace — but it is a number printed in a money column, and §7.2's first bullet admits no exception, so it was the one figure in the pack the gate's own first rule was not true of. `buildMattersBlock` records it against `buildMatters.quantum.<rank>`; the source must be unique per matter, because one source issued twice with two values is what `assertConsistent` throws on.
- **The comparative is the period the pack CLAIMS it is** (R-P55). `PackPagesDocument.comparativePeriod` carries BOTH the printed `label` and the `start`/`end` window the prior figures were read over, because the two fail differently: the provenance check (a `prior` cell must come from a `.prior` source) is fully satisfied by a document whose data is right and whose heading names the wrong six months, and a director reads the heading. The gate re-derives the label from the window through `formatPackPeriod` and refuses a drift, refuses a comparative column with no stated period, and refuses a stated period no statement compares against. `null` means a first-period company — page 12 then says so in words. The label is printed from the ONE field (page 12's basis note, and a footnote under every statement carrying a prior column); a second formatting of the same dates is how the page and the gate start disagreeing. `formatPackDate`/`formatPackPeriod` moved to `pages/packPeriodLabel.ts` so the composer can use them without importing the fetcher; `boardPackDocument.ts` re-exports both.
- **A quotient must state its working, both ways.** The gate refuses a `money` figure that carries a numerator and denominator (nothing to recompute) AND a `pct`/`ratio` figure that carries none — the second was the bigger hole: seven live figures, current ratio, cash ratio and debt-to-equity among them, were never recomputed because the check only fired on figures that had already volunteered their working. `readOverCurrentLiabilities` (`packMetricReads.ts`) supplies the basis for the two liquidity ratios and reports them as NOT MEANINGFUL when there are no current liabilities: `safeDiv` returns 0 on a zero denominator, and "0.00 cover" says the opposite of the truth about a company that owes nothing short-term. `days` is deliberately not a quotient format — a day count is a rate times a period length, with no numerator-over-denominator to reproduce. Ratio tolerance is **0.0051**: `reportingEngineService` rounds a ratio TWICE (`safeDiv` to 4dp, then `r2` to 2dp), so an honest ratio can sit 0.00505 from the true quotient and a flat half-unit refuses it — but no more than that, because a ratio prints to two decimals and 0.009 of error shows 0.73 where the truth is 0.74. Pinned from BOTH sides, so neither tightening nor widening it passes.
- **EBITDA is an identity; the operating line is not.** EBITDA is `ebit + dep + amort` and the pack hands `toFinancialData` no depreciation or amortisation, so it must equal the operating line — pinned, and it FAILS loudly if a depreciation field is ever added, which is the correct alarm. Operating profit is READ from the classifier, and the classifier's answer is not the naive formula: on the demo figures it is 735,000 where revenue − cost of sales + other income − operating expenses gives 750,000, the 15,000 being other expenses. Asserting the formula would fail an honest pack; what holds the line together is that every page reads it through `OPERATING_PROFIT_SOURCE`, which `assertConsistent` enforces.
- **A named plug that dwarfs its subject must be sized in words.** Both bridges self-plug, so `checkWaterfall` can never fire on real data — and page 8's plug is R266,000 against a net cash movement of R124,000, more than twice the thing it explains. `disproportionatePlugSentence` (`packWaterfallIntegrity.ts`) fires at **10%** of the SMALLER of the endpoint and the movement, and the gate refuses a pack that draws a disproportionate plug without carrying that exact sentence. A disclosure rather than a refusal, because the gap is in the FIGURE SET (no field carries interest, tax, or cash reconciling items) and refusing would leave an ordinary company unable to render a pack at all. The bar is marked `uncaptured: true` rather than matched by label, so re-wording either plug cannot silence the check.
- **A tile with no movement says WHICH of three things is missing.** `PackKpiTile.deltaAbsence` (`noComparativePeriod` / `noPriorValue` / `unusableBase` / `noCurrentValue`) is non-null exactly when `delta` is null. One null used to carry all of them, so page 3 printed "No comparative period" eight times on a pack whose statement pages carried a prior column and whose page 12 named the comparative's dates — prior revenue was nil and prior net profit was (905), so `marginPct` refused the BASE, not the period. `unusableBase` prints `NOT_A_POSITIVE_BASE`, the same string the statement tables put under their n/m cells. The page's own "Comparison basis" note reads `comparativePeriod` — the document's one answer — rather than restating it.
- **A page may not name a chart the pack does not draw.** `pages/packTrendCrossReference.ts` derives both halves of the month-versus-period reconciliation from the panels `buildTrendPanels` actually returned: `trendWindowNote` for page 10, `periodWindowFootnotes` for the income statement and scorecard pages that carry the period figure. The first version was one hard-coded sentence about gross margin, and it printed on a pack that draws no gross-margin panel, two centimetres above the disclosure explaining why. The reciprocal is a page FOOTNOTE and not a disclosure block because the two pages that need it are the fullest in the pack — as a headed block it put the income statement 4.8mm and the scorecard 12.2mm past the paper.
- **A chart gives a rung back when its page has something to say — ONE rung.** Page 7's composition bar (negative total assets) and page 6's profit bridge (a plug past `UNCAPTURED_SHARE_LIMIT`) both step from `full` to `half`. Page 7 first stepped to `inline`, and at 48mm the y-axis had 7.7px between tick centres at 12px type: `(1 000)`, `(2 000)` and `(3 000)` printed on top of each other. `packChartAxisLegibility.test.ts` reads the tick coordinates out of the SVG and refuses a gap smaller than the type.
- **A mid-statement subtotal bar is `kind: 'subtotal'`** (R-P50). It is drawn from the axis, so the chain restarts there; the gate checks its `end` against what the bars accumulated to, never its `start`. Reporting it by drawn direction — as the model did before — makes the gate fail an honest bridge, which teaches everyone to bypass it.
